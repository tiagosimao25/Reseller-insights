How it works

Reseller Insights doesn't store a prewritten answer for every situation. It turns your inputs into a handful of 0–100 scores, then composes the diagnosis, next action and email from small, independently-triggered rules — which is what lets a compact model produce thousands of distinct outcomes instead of a lookup table of canned answers.

Pipeline

From raw fields to a recommendation

Every "Generate Diagnosis" click runs the same five-stage pipeline. Nothing here is random — the same inputs always produce the same output, and every number on screen can be traced back to a field you typed in.

Core mechanic

Weighted averages that renormalize around missing data

You said yourself that not every field will always be available — that's the norm, not the exception, for reseller data. So instead of requiring every field, each score is a weighted average of whatever inputs you provided, with the weights of the missing ones redistributed proportionally across the rest.

Worked example: the Growth score normally blends five signals — ARR Growth (30%), Sales Growth (25%), NSB Δ (20%), Licenses Δ (15%), End Users Δ (10%). If you only supply ARR Growth and Sales Growth, the engine doesn't average them against three zeros — it renormalizes: (0.30 × ARR + 0.25 × Sales) ÷ (0.30 + 0.25), so those two inputs carry their relative weight (55% and 45%) rather than being diluted by data you never gave it. A score only becomes No data when every one of its inputs is missing.

Special cases

Deriving Renewal Rate from Agreements counts

Renewal Rate is the one field the engine insists on having, one way or another — either type the % directly, or supply the Agreements counts (Total, Renewed, Partial, Not Renewed) and it's derived: first from Renewed ÷ Total, or if only Not Renewed is known, from (Total − Not Renewed − Partial) ÷ Total. Total itself is filled in from Renewed + Partial + Not Renewed if you leave it blank.

When the rate is derived this way, the Not-Renewed and Partial ratios are excluded from Renewal Health's weighted average rather than added on top — they were built from the exact same three numbers as the rate itself, so including both would double-count one data point instead of blending two independent signals.

Value-range renewal risk

The optional renewal-rate-by-value-range breakdown ($0–1k through $50k+) isn't just displayed — it's read for where the risk is concentrated, because a weak renewal rate driven by small accounts is a different problem than one driven by large ones:

  • Systemic — both a low-value band and a high-value band are under 70%. Broad-based weakness, heaviest penalty.
  • High-value concentration — the worst band is $10k+ and under 75%. Smaller volume of accounts, disproportionate revenue impact.
  • Low-value concentration — only bands under $10k are weak. Larger volume, smaller revenue impact, lightest penalty.

    Why this produces thousands of distinct outputs from a small rule set

    The Diagnosis block works differently from Next Action: instead of stopping at the first match, it collects every condition that fires, independently. There are roughly 17 such conditions across renewal, growth, size, auto-renew and upsell, each able to land in 2–4 different states depending on the data you provide (or stay silent if that data is missing). Combined, that's enough to generate diagnostic bullet-sets in the thousands without a single sentence being written twice — the variety comes from composition, not from an ever-growing library of pre-written paragraphs. The suggested email and product recommendation are built the same way, assembled from a handful of conditional paragraphs rather than templated per scenario.
