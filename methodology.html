How it works

No canned answers. Your numbers become a few scores (0–100), and those scores automatically write the diagnosis, the next step, and the email below them.

The first few sections explain the logic in plain terms. Everything under Reference further down is exact numbers for double-checking — skip it unless you need it.

Pipeline

5 steps, every time

Every "Generate Diagnosis" click runs the same 5 steps. Same numbers in, same result out — nothing here is random or guessed, and every result on screen traces back to a field you filled in.

Core mechanic

What if I don't have every number?

That's normal — you won't always have every field, and that's fine. When something's missing, the tool leans more on the numbers you did give it, instead of marking the reseller down for a gap in your data.

Example: Growth normally blends 5 signals. If you only fill in 2 of them, those 2 simply carry all the weight between them — the missing 3 are skipped, not counted as zero. A score only shows "No data" when every single one of its inputs is empty.

Component scores

Renewal Health

How likely is this reseller to renew — and is that a solid renewal, or a shaky one propped up by luck? Mainly driven by the Renewal Rate and CLM status, plus how bad any recent non-renewals were.

CLM active scores 80, not active scores 30. If renewals are especially weak in one deal-size range (more on that below), a penalty is subtracted too: .

Growth

Is the business growing or shrinking? Blends up to 5 signals — ARR, Sales, New Business (NSB), Licenses, End Users. No change scores 50 (neutral); growing faster than expected pushes the score up, shrinking pulls it down.

Each signal moves the score by a different amount per 1% change — a swing in one volatile quarter means something different than the same swing smoothed over 12 months:

Auto-Renew

Is passive churn covered? If you give a country or Europe benchmark, this reseller's auto-renew % is compared against it — above benchmark scores well, below it doesn't. No benchmark on hand? We just use the raw auto-renew % as the score.

Reseller Size

How much does this account actually matter? Based on its share of total country sales (or licenses, if sales aren't available). A big reseller in trouble is a bigger deal than a small one, so the score is stretched to match the Small / Medium / Large / Strategic labels — a Strategic reseller (20%+ share) scores near the top, not a third of the way there:

Upsell Opportunity

How many licenses are ready for an upgrade — Acrobat Standard/Pro → Studio, Creative Cloud Pro → Pro Plus, or Pro Plus → Enterprise Edition 4? The more eligible licenses relative to the total license count, the higher the score. No license total to compare against? We fall back to a capped estimate, so a handful of upgrades can never look like a huge opportunity on its own.

Overall Priority

The one number that answers "who do I deal with first?" — combines every score above, weighted by how urgent and how big the impact is:

Renewal Risk and the Auto-Renew Gap count backwards here — a reseller in trouble pushes this number up, since fixing a problem is more urgent than maintaining something that's already healthy.

Special cases

No Renewal Rate on hand? We can work it out.

Renewal Rate is the one number we really need. Don't have it typed up? Give us the Agreements counts instead — Total, Renewed, Partial, Not Renewed — and we'll calculate it for you. (Missing the Total? We'll add up Renewed + Partial + Not Renewed for you too.)

One thing we're careful about: when we calculate the rate this way, we don't also penalize Renewal Health again for those same non-renewals — that would count the same problem twice.

Where are the renewal losses coming from?

If you fill in the renewal rate by deal size ($0–1k through $50k+), we check where the weak spots are — losing small deals is a different problem than losing your biggest accounts:

  • Weak everywhere — both your small deals and your big deals are under 70%. The most serious pattern, and the biggest penalty.
  • Weak on your biggest accounts — small deals are fine, but $10k+ accounts are under 75%. Fewer accounts affected, but a bigger revenue hit.
  • Weak on your smaller accounts — only deals under $10k are soft. More accounts affected, but each one matters less.

How "Next Action" gets picked

Once the scores exist, we check a short list of rules from top to bottom and stop at the first one that matches — not a giant table of pre-written answers for every possible combination:

    Nothing above matches? The fallback is simple: keep monitoring, no action needed right now.

    Why every reseller gets a different write-up

    Diagnosis, the email, and the product pick aren't chosen from a list of pre-written answers — they're built line by line from this reseller's actual numbers. About 17 independent checks run every time (on renewal, growth, size, auto-renew, and upsell), each landing in 2–4 possible states depending on what you typed in. Combined, that's enough to produce thousands of different write-ups from a small set of rules — the variety comes from mixing and matching, not from an ever-growing pile of pre-written paragraphs.
